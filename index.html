Mermazine: For the Merfolk of East Anglia!

We first heard their siren call in the spring of 2019 and, the more we sought them, the more they began to emerge from obscurity: the beautiful, the grotesque, the perfect, the defaced, the badly restored, the lovingly created. Some might have been intended to convey Christian strictures about the sin of vanity, but this was only one tiny aspect of the wealth of meaning these wonderful creatures have carried across the centuries, much of which will be explored in depth in this and future issues of Mermazine.

Although we made many mermaid pilgrimages this summer, seven years ago by the time the days were shortening, we realised we had only scratched the surface of East Anglian mermaid lore. Perhaps we needed the intervening time period of the pandemic and beyond, to realise that this was too vast a topic to be compressed into book form. The merfolk seemed so much more from us than that, for one lay theme running through all that we had discovered was that of creative inspiration. Merfolk call us to write poetry, stories, learned articles, to draw and paint, to embroider, to make jewellery, to protest about the pollution of their waters.

With the launch of issue one, we are making a start, and Alice, Chris and Val hope that you too will be inspired by a glimpse in the mermaid's mirror, a flash of bright scales or the touch of a comb through your hair. Meet the Mermazine team!

Val Thomas

Val has a keen interest in witchcraft, spells, literature, and traditional crafts. She writes, practises as a medical and magical herbalist, spins, weaves and teaches, as well as continuing with her own explorations of the realms of the Gods, the Fair Ones and other spiritual beings.

Val lives in Norfolk and loves sharing her enthusiasm for the county's stories and traditions, its plants and creatures and for the magic which thrives here in this sacred landscape.
